---
title: Manifesto
summary: "Programming is also putting the body into it: faith lives in the practice, not in immediate success."
---

**I.** An error doesn't paralyze: it multiplies paths. Debugging is a mantra — fail, adjust, begin again — and the point is not arriving, but sustaining the process.

**II.** Any craft is a vehicle toward something deeper. The tension of the bow suspiciously resembles the pressure of fingers typing at two in the morning: concentration, rhythm, setting the ego aside.

**III.** Work turns into confidence, and confidence into dynamic relaxation. One day thought gets ahead of the fingers and flow appears effortlessly: the body thinks for itself.

**IV.** The infinite loop has an exit: the error as opportunity. The feature you weren't looking for that now makes sense. The poetics of domesticated entropy.

**V.** Writing and programming are the same gesture: chipping away at the silence by typing until the screen becomes a skin to rest whispers on.

**VI.** The only failure is stopping. If it goes well, you learn; if it goes wrong, you learn even more.

**VII.** Programming is also putting the body into it. It's not just solving problems: it's enduring. My faith lives in the practice — in the infinite loop of trying once more, running it again with philosophical dignity, and seeing what happens.

*With echoes of Ray Bradbury (*Zen in the Art of Writing*) and Leticia Obeid (*Galería de copias*).*
